# Office characters: `characters.png` and `characters.json`

The Office cast atlas, drawn with [PixelLab](https://www.pixellab.ai) through its MCP tools in variant B's style (the chibi Claude-1 approved on 2026-09-25 after two style rounds). It holds the nine characters `charFor` in `src/office/config.ts` names: Claude-1, Frontend-dev-1, Me-1, dev-1, dev-2, employee-1, employee-2, employee-3 and security-audit-1. The atlas is 672 x 783 with 126 frames. Each character's colours follow the prototype's `PAL` (skin, hair, top, trousers), with one silhouette cue each: a beard, headphones, top bun, ponytail, curls, glasses or a high-visibility vest. Me-1 is the exception. It is the orchestrator mascot, redrawn on 2026-09-25 as a stylized pixel character (see Me-1 below), so its colours no longer match its `PAL` entry. employee-2 is the final-size pilot of 2026-09-28: its frames are generated at the cell's own size and copied 1:1, not resampled, and its head and legs are then restored from the resampled frames for design (see employee-2 below). dev-1, dev-2, employee-1 and employee-3 are final size since 2026-09-28 too, the part-1 batch of four regenerated the same way (see dev-1, dev-2, employee-1 and employee-3 below); employee-1's raw regeneration drifted on hair and cardigan colour and was corrected with a head transplant and a hue shift, both pixel edits.

## Frame contract

- Frames are 48 x 87, drawn facing left (the engine mirrors them for right). The anchor is bottom-centre (`anchor: {x: 0.5, y: 1}` in the JSON), and the feet stand on row 82, 4 px above the frame bottom. Every idle and type frame's lowest opaque row is row 82. A walk cycle's lowest row across its four frames is row 82; in the other frames a lifted foot ends above it. Rows 83 to 86 are empty in every frame. So are column 0, column 47 and row 0, so a frame never touches its neighbour in the atlas.
- Keys are `<char>/<walk|type|idle>/<front|rear>/<n>`: `walk` 0 to 3, `type` 0 and 1, `idle` 0, each in `front` and `rear`. That is 14 frames per character.
- `characters.json` is a TexturePacker hash (`frames`, `meta.image`, `meta.size`), which `Assets.load` reads as a Pixi `Spritesheet`.
- Layout: one row of 14 cells per character, top to bottom in the order listed above. Each row runs walk front 0 to 3, walk rear 0 to 3, type front 0 and 1, type rear 0 and 1, idle front, idle rear.

## How the frames were made

- **Character:** `create_character`, standard mode, `n_directions` 8, `size` 128 (a 180 x 180 canvas), `view` high top-down, `outline` single color black outline, `shading` detailed shading, `detail` high detail, `proportions` chibi preset. These are Claude-1 variant B's settings. `front` is the south-west rotation, `rear` the north-west one. The idle frames are those rotations.
- **Walk:** `animate_character` with `template_animation_id` walking-4-frames, directions south-west and north-west.
- **Type (seated typing):** `animate_with_skeleton_v3`, view high top-down, 3 frames, `first_frame_url` set to the character's rotation for that direction. Frame 0 is used; frame 2 repeats frame 0's pose. The generated frame 1 was used until 2026-09-29; since then `type/<view>/1` is frame 0 with only the hands moved (see Keystroke pass below).
  - The skeleton for south-west (`front`) is round 2's (job 8216667f): knees bent and hands forward.
  - The skeleton for north-west (`rear`) is the desk-height one of 2026-09-26 (`direction` north-west, the tool's "Facing of the sprite (south = towards the camera)"). The first rear skeleton held the hands at lap height, 11 to 21 px above the feet, while the keyboards of the back-to-camera seats sit 38 px up and one walk-grid cell ahead, so seated typists typed 15 to 40 px short of them. The legs and hips are unchanged. The neck and shoulders lean about 0.06 forward and 0.03 up, with the head. Both arms reach forward and up with level forearms, and only the wrists move between the two frames. Those frames sat the figure sunk into the chair; the Upright pass below raises it.
  - Rear keypoints (x, y as canvas fractions, Claude-1's frame; `kp2.mjs` and `kp4.mjs` in `office-rear-typing-work/` take the same dy and scale arguments as `kp.mjs nw`): nose 0.32, 0.415; neck 0.44, 0.48; right shoulder 0.5, 0.49; left shoulder 0.4, 0.51; hips, knees and ankles as the first rear skeleton (right 0.55, 0.7 / 0.47, 0.67 / 0.47, 0.77; left 0.48, 0.71 / 0.4, 0.68 / 0.4, 0.78); eyes 0.315, 0.355 and 0.345, 0.365; ears 0.525, 0.385 and 0.435, 0.415.

## Night readability

The room background, night-window layer and entities use the same scene colour matrix. At d = 1 it transforms the carpet colors (`#646d80` / `#5f687b`) to `#202433` / `#1e2231` (the darker night base of 2026-09-28; the earlier matrix gave `#2b3152` / `#292f4f`). For each character, the table gives the share of opaque pixels within RGB distance 24 of those carpet colors, by day and at night, over every atlas frame. A darker base puts more colours within that fixed distance, so the night shares rose with it.

- By day, every character is at 0.2% or below.
- The largest night overlaps are security-audit-1’s grey hair and shadows (34.1%, 16.2% under the earlier matrix) and employee-1’s purple shirt (11.9%); both characters keep their black outline, and a lamp pool lifts the floor around a lit seat.
- Me-1 palette adjustment overlaps the transformed carpet by 3.5% overall.
- The first employee-2 candidate had khaki chinos; the resampled art used cream chinos and had 0.6% overall overlap with the transformed carpet. The final-size pilot's frames have 0.0% by day and at night.
