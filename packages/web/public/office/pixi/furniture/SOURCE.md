# Office furniture for the Pixi room (layouts 1c and 2)

Pixel-art furniture and props for the `FURN` list in `packages/web/src/office/pixi/world.ts`, plus a ceiling lamp, in the variant B style the user approved on 2026-09-25 (warm-wood writing desk, chibi characters). The art was generated with PixelLab through its MCP tools and then edited by deterministic scripts: flips, crops, projection fixes, slice and band cuts, recolours and small hand-placed details. No file is committed as a raw download.

The engine places layout 2 (see [Layout 2](#layout-2)). Of the layout 1c files it still places the orchestrator's desk, monitors and chair, the keyboard and mouse, the monitor back, the plants and the lamps. The layout 1c worker and review desks and chairs (`desk-worker.png`, `desk-review.png`, `chair-worker.png`, `chair-review.png`) were no longer placed and were deleted when the orchestrator's desk and chair were redrawn at one pixel size, since nothing is built from them any more. The lab bench, coffee counter and water cooler files were deleted with layout 1c, and the printer files when the QA wall screen took over its running state and pass/fail mark.

## Placement

- Every file is pixel art with a transparent background and binary alpha. Scale it with `nearest` only (`scaleMode: 'nearest'` on the texture).
- The world projection is the handoff's `p(i, j, z) = [696 + (i − j)·48, 216 + (i + j)·24 − z·48]`. One tile is 96 × 48 px and one z unit is 48 px. Every piece is drawn at world scale: one art pixel is one world pixel. No file is resized by a factor other than a whole number or 1/whole number; the pieces in [One pixel size](#one-pixel-size) are generated or drawn at their final size and used at 1:1.
- Outlines: a piece's silhouette is inked, a 1 px outline darker than luminance 60 (most pieces use `#1b1f27`). The pieces redrawn at one pixel size keep the generation's own dark outline and set every lighter silhouette pixel to `#1b1f27`, without growing the file.
- Each file has an anchor `(ax, ay)`: the pixel position, from the top-left corner of the file, that sits on the file's world point. Draw the sprite with its top-left corner at `p(world point) − (ax, ay)`. In Pixi, that is `sprite.anchor.set(ax / width, ay / height)` at `p(world point)`.
- For a box-like piece (desks, QA desks, the meeting table, kitchen counter, fridge, sofa, coffee table), the world point is the footprint's front corner `p(i + w, j + d, 0)`: the lowest corner of the footprint on screen. The anchor is that corner's position in the file, so the art sits on its tiles. For a small prop (plants, floor lamp), the world point is the footprint centre `p(i + w/2, j + d/2, 0)` and the anchor is the centre of the prop's base.
- Depth keys follow the handoff: `zIndex = round((i + j) × 100)` for the point named in the table. Furniture uses its footprint centre. A part that sits on a desk uses the desk's key + 1 (the keyboard and mouse) or + 2 (a monitor, which stands in front of the keyboard), so it draws after the desk and after anyone seated behind it, and before a front-row sitter in front of the desk. A chair uses a point 0.15 tile behind the seat spot against the seat's facing, so it draws before a person who faces the camera and after one whose back is to it.
- A `-lit` file is an overlay for night. It has the same size and anchor as its base file, and it holds only the pixels that give light, in their lit colours. Draw it outside the night colour matrix (not through it) with normal blending, at alpha = night share `n` (a seat screen's at 0.6 `n`), in the depth order just after its base file (the base key + 0.5), so anyone in front of the piece still covers it. The engine uses one shared smooth radial texture for its additive light pools and glows. Three room masks keep floor pools inside main (including the kitchen and lounge), the QA corner (`lab`) and the meeting room (`review`); small sconce halos stay on their back walls.
- Screen overlays (`monitor-back-lit.png`, `monitor-front-lit.png`, `monitors-orch-lit.png`, `qa-desk-lit.png`) follow the handoff's screen rule: show them only while the seat in front of the screen is taken by someone working or verifying. The orchestrator's `monitors-orch-lit.png` follows the same rule. Screens are off at empty seats: `monitor-front.png` and `qa-desk.png` are replaced by their `-off` files while no character has arrived at the seat (see [Off screens](#off-screens)). `monitor-back.png` and `monitors-orch.png` show the backs of their monitors, with no screen visible to the camera, so they are unchanged and have no off file. The other overlays (lamps and the QA wall screen's overlay for the state it shows) follow the night share alone.
